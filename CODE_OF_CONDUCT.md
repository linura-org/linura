# Code of Conduct

Linura expects technical discussion to be rigorous, respectful, specific, and focused on the work. Strong disagreement about architecture, security, implementation, or product direction is welcome; harassment and personal abuse are not.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- critique claims, designs, code, tests, evidence, and decisions rather than people;
- assume technical disagreement can be legitimate and ask for evidence when a claim matters;
- respect privacy, coordinated security disclosure, and confidential conduct reports;
- avoid disruptive repetition, intimidation, threats, discrimination, or targeted harassment;
- disclose material conflicts of interest when participating in a decision they may influence;
- follow maintainer moderation intended to restore productive participation.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, discrimination, stalking, threats, or personal attacks;
- sexualized harassment or unwanted sexual attention;
- publication of another person's private information without permission;
- deliberate disruption of technical discussion or project infrastructure;
- impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly fabricated evidence;
- retaliation against someone for making a good-faith conduct or security report.

Good-faith technical criticism, including criticism of Linura, its maintainers, or accepted decisions, is not a conduct violation merely because it is strong or uncomfortable.

## Scope

This policy applies to Linura-controlled repositories, Issues, Discussions, pull requests, review threads, official project communication channels, and project events or interactions where a participant is representing Linura.

It does not grant the project authority over unrelated private conduct, but credible off-platform behavior may be considered when it creates a concrete safety or participation risk in Linura spaces.

## Reporting

Do not use a public issue to report private conduct concerns.

Report conduct concerns privately to **conduct@linura.org**. Include relevant links, dates, participants, screenshots or logs when appropriate, and any immediate safety concern.

Security vulnerabilities follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not the conduct process.

## Handling and confidentiality

Conduct reports should be shared only with people needed to assess and resolve the report. Reporters, subjects, and witnesses should not be promised absolute confidentiality when legal, platform-safety, or immediate-risk obligations require disclosure, but unnecessary disclosure is prohibited.

A maintainer who is the subject of a report must not be the sole decision-maker for that report when another suitable independent responder is available. During the sole-maintainer phase, a conflict that cannot be handled independently may require escalation to the relevant hosting platform or another mutually acceptable independent reviewer.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity, pattern, intent, and risk, maintainers may:

1. give a private or public clarification or warning;
2. require behavior to stop or content to be corrected;
3. temporarily restrict participation;
4. remove comments or other material when necessary to restore safety or productive discussion;
5. block participation in project spaces;
6. revoke project privileges or maintainership under the governance process;
7. escalate credible threats, platform abuse, or unlawful conduct to the appropriate service provider or authority.

Enforcement actions should be proportionate and documented internally. Public explanations should disclose only what is necessary.

## Appeals and mistakes

A participant may request reconsideration by replying through the private reporting channel with new information or a concrete reason the action was mistaken or disproportionate.

Moderation decisions can be corrected. Retaliation, public pressure campaigns against reporters, or attempts to expose confidential report material are not valid appeal mechanisms.

## Project authority

This Code of Conduct governs participation; it does not override Linura's technical governance, security invariants, licensing, or legal obligations.

# Sponsorship disclosure and reporting

Linura should make material sponsorship understandable without turning public project documentation into a ledger of confidential contracts.

The goal is to make the relationship, funded purpose, and authority boundary visible while keeping exact financial, tax, security, and personal information in the systems where it belongs.

## Disclosure baseline

For each material institutional sponsor, Linura should normally disclose:

- sponsor name;
- sponsorship class;
- sponsorship term;
- funded area or named program;
- whether support is cash, in-kind, or mixed;
- recognition provided;
- public evidence or program status;
- any material conflict disclosure required by governance.

The agreement may disclose the exact amount, an amount band, or keep the sponsor-specific amount private. Linura should still maintain exact internal accounting records and should publish aggregate annual project funding totals when practical and legally appropriate.

## What remains private

The public registry should not expose:

- bank or payment details;
- invoices or tax identifiers;
- credentials;
- private addresses or personal financial information;
- confidential contract terms unrelated to public project influence;
- security-sensitive infrastructure details;
- embargoed vulnerability information;
- private conduct reports.

- internal sponsor targets, contact notes, warm-introduction paths, ask ranges, and negotiation strategy;
- privileged legal advice and draft counsel communications.

Public sponsorship reporting is project transparency, not the corporation's CRM, tax ledger, board record, or complete set of commercial books.

## In-kind support

Material in-kind support should describe what was provided and why it matters.

Examples include:

- GPU or workstation hardware;
- CI or cloud credits;
- dedicated test infrastructure;
- security review time;
- engineering support;
- travel or event support.

Linura should avoid publishing misleading nominal valuations. If an estimated value is useful, the estimation method should be stated or the contribution should be reported without a monetary value.

## Quarterly reporting

While Linura has active institutional sponsorship, publish a concise quarterly sponsorship report when practical.

A report should contain:

### Funding and relationships

- active sponsors and their classes;
- active funded programs;
- new or ended partnerships relevant to sponsored work;
- aggregate funds received during the quarter when appropriate;
- material in-kind contributions.

### Engineering outcomes

- completed deliverables;
- qualification evidence produced;
- security or reliability improvements;
- releases or documentation materially enabled by funding;
- work that failed, changed scope, or remains blocked.

### Independence and conflicts

- material sponsor-related conflict disclosures;
- any request that Linura declined because it conflicted with architecture, security, governance, or project independence;
- confirmation that no sponsor received architecture, merge, release, or security-exception authority.

### Next quarter

- active program goals;
- known dependencies;
- important risks;
- planned evidence/review milestones.

## Annual summary

At least once per year while material sponsorship is active, Linura should publish an annual summary covering:

- aggregate project funding received;
- material in-kind support;
- active and completed funded programs;
- major outcomes;
- significant failures or lessons;
- sponsor/partner conflicts disclosed during the year;
- the current receiving entity or fiscal-host model at a high level.

The annual summary is project transparency, not audited financial statements unless Linura explicitly says otherwise.

After a corporate steward exists, public project reporting should identify that steward accurately at a high level without implying nonprofit or charitable tax status.

## Sponsor registry template

The public sponsor registry can use a compact record such as:

```text
Sponsor: Example Corp
Class: Strategic Sponsor
Term: 2027-01-01 — 2027-12-31
Support: cash + test infrastructure
Program: Workstation and Platform Qualification
Disclosure: amount band by agreement
Recognition: sponsors page + release acknowledgements
Status: active
Evidence: <links>
Authority: no technical or governance authority granted
```

## Program closeout

When a material funded program ends, publish a closeout note when practical that answers:

- What was funded?
- What shipped or qualified?
- What did not work?
- What evidence exists?
- What remains unresolved?
- Did the scope or sponsor relationship create any disclosed conflict?
- Is follow-on funding proposed?

A successful sponsorship report does not need to claim that every technical objective succeeded. Accurate negative evidence is preferable to sponsor-friendly fiction.

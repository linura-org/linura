# Sponsorship standard terms baseline

This document is a public policy baseline for future Linura sponsorship agreements. It is **not** a contract, legal advice, or a substitute for counsel in the jurisdiction of the verified receiving entity and sponsor.

A real sponsorship agreement must identify the legal parties, governing law, tax treatment, payment route, signatories, and any required compliance terms.

## 1. Parties and purpose

The agreement should identify:

- the verified legal or fiscal recipient receiving funds for Linura work;
- the sponsoring organization;
- the sponsorship class or funded program, if any;
- the term;
- the public purpose of the sponsorship.

The agreement must not describe the sponsor as an owner, controller, board member, maintainer, or architectural authority unless a separate lawful relationship independently establishes that role.

## 2. Contribution

The agreement should state:

- cash amount and currency, or the nature and fair description of in-kind support;
- payment schedule;
- invoice/payment method;
- applicable taxes, fees, and transaction costs;
- refund or non-refundable status where lawful;
- treatment of unused program funds at the end of the term.

## 3. Scope and funded outcomes

Where funding supports a named engineering program, the agreement should define:

- intended outcomes;
- explicit exclusions;
- evidence/reporting expectations;
- material dependencies;
- term and review points.

A funded outcome is a target, not a guarantee that a particular implementation will be merged or released.

## 4. Technical and governance independence

The agreement must preserve the project boundary defined by `GOVERNANCE.md` and `docs/community/sponsorship.md`.

Sponsorship does not grant:

- architectural decision authority;
- roadmap vetoes;
- merge, release, maintainer, or repository-admin rights;
- security-policy exceptions;
- privileged access to confidential vulnerability or conduct information;
- undisclosed preferential treatment;
- a private authority path around normal Linura controls.

The sponsor may provide requirements, use cases, test environments, feedback, or engineering input. Linura decides whether and how that input is compatible with the project.

## 5. Open-source and intellectual-property boundary

Unless a separate agreement explicitly and lawfully says otherwise:

- contributions to the public Linura repository follow the repository's applicable open-source license and contribution policy;
- sponsorship does not transfer ownership of the Linura project, name, marks, domains, repository, or governance;
- sponsorship does not give the sponsor an exclusive license to public Linura code;
- sponsor-funded work intended for the public project should be developed through normal public project processes when security/confidentiality constraints permit.

Any private deliverable, proprietary integration, trademark license, or separate commercial IP arrangement requires an explicit commercial agreement and must not be inferred from sponsorship.

## 6. Recognition and logo use

The agreement may grant Linura a limited, revocable right to use the sponsor's approved name and logo solely for agreed sponsorship recognition.

The agreement may grant the sponsor a limited right to state that it supports Linura, subject to accurate wording and brand guidelines.

Neither party receives a general endorsement right.

## 7. Reporting and transparency

Material sponsorship should follow `docs/community/sponsor-reporting.md`.

The agreement should state what may be disclosed publicly, including:

- sponsor identity;
- sponsorship class;
- funded area/program;
- contribution amount, band, or confidential status;
- term;
- public evidence and outcome reporting.

Linura should not accept terms that require hidden technical influence or undisclosed governance privileges.

## 8. Confidentiality

Confidentiality should be narrow and purpose-specific.

It may cover legitimate business, financial, pre-release, or security-sensitive information. It must not be used to hide technical authority, undisclosed conflicts, security-policy exceptions, or misleading public claims.

Embargoed vulnerability information follows `SECURITY.md`, not ordinary sponsor access.

## 9. Security and access

Sponsorship does not automatically create production access, repository administration, credentials, secret access, or access to private security reports.

Any operational access required for a funded program must be separately authorized, least-privilege, time-bounded where practical, and auditable.

## 10. Compliance

The final agreement should contain the compliance terms required by the parties and receiving jurisdiction, which may include:

- anti-bribery and anti-corruption;
- sanctions/export-control compliance;
- tax and invoicing representations;
- data protection;
- conflicts of interest;
- procurement/vendor requirements.

Linura may decline a sponsorship whose compliance obligations cannot be satisfied safely or proportionately.

## 11. No warranty of technical outcome

Sponsorship funds engineering work and project capacity. It does not warrant that:

- a proposed feature will be accepted;
- a target platform will qualify;
- an experiment will succeed;
- a security review will find no issues;
- a release will occur on a sponsor's preferred date.

Negative evidence is still valid project evidence.

## 12. Termination

The agreement should define termination rights for material breach, legal/compliance risk, non-payment, misuse of marks, security risk, or material reputation/independence risk.

Termination should also define what happens to recognition, unused funds, outstanding invoices, confidential information, and funded work already completed.

## 13. Liability, warranties, indemnities, and governing law

These clauses must be negotiated and reviewed by qualified counsel for the actual parties and jurisdiction.

Linura's public policy does not pre-select governing law, dispute forum, liability caps, indemnities, or tax characterization before the receiving entity exists.

## 14. Signatures

Only an authorized signatory of the verified receiving party may execute a sponsorship agreement.

Project maintainers should not sign in the name of a nonexistent Linura legal entity.
